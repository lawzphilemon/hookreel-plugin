# WTF Hook 999 — Distilled Framework

Use this reference to choose a hook mechanism. Do not copy examples blindly; adapt them to the user's actual audience, evidence, and content payoff.

## Three emotional pillars

- **GAIN**: desired reward, progress, status, relief, opportunity.
- **PAIN**: loss, risk, frustration, wasted effort, delayed consequence.
- **CURIOSITY**: unresolved contradiction, hidden mechanism, unexpected comparison.

## Eight basic human instincts

Choose only the instincts genuinely connected to the topic. Never force all eight.

1. Health and illness
2. Hunger and satiety
3. Sleep and recovery
4. Money and luxury
5. Life after death / mortality
6. Sex and relationships
7. Children's welfare / family security
8. Pride, status, and self-importance

## 15 hook types

| # | Type | Core formula | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | GOOD = BAD | `[Hal yang dianggap baik] malah [akibat buruk]` | Flip a conventional positive belief with a defensible explanation. |
| 02 | CRAZY MATH | `[Angka spesifik] dibanding [angka/hasil kontras]` | Use verifiable numbers, scale shock, speed shock, or comparison shock. |
| 03 | BROKEN LABEL | `[Label/status] tapi [kenyataan kontradiktif]` | Break stereotypes, credentials, or self-labels. |
| 04 | SILENT KILLER | `[Kebiasaan normal] diam-diam [kerusakan]` | Reveal an invisible, cumulative downside without fear-mongering. |
| 05 | TIME SHOCK | `[Hasil besar/kecil] dalam [waktu tak terduga]` | Transformations, stagnation, delay, urgency. |
| 06 | DARK HORSE | `[Background diremehkan] tapi [pencapaian tinggi]` | Underdog, hope, unexpected winner. |
| 07 | FORBIDDEN TRUTH | `Yang jarang dibicarakan: [kebenaran defensible]` | Name an uncomfortable but supportable truth. |
| 08 | REVERSE REALITY | `Bukan [X] yang menentukan [Y], tapi [kebalikannya]` | Reframe causal assumptions. |
| 09 | CONFESSION | `Saya akui, saya [kelemahan/masa lalu relatable]` | Personal vulnerability with a useful payoff. |
| 10 | SYSTEM GLITCH | `Kamu bukan gagal; [sistem/proses] yang salah desain` | Expose process or incentive failures without scapegoating. |
| 11 | PATTERN BREAK | `[X], [X], [X] — ternyata [kebalikan]` | Establish a pattern, then break it. |
| 12 | ABSURD COMPARISON | `[X remeh] setara/lebih kuat dari [Y diagungkan]` | Force two incompatible mental frames into one comparison. |
| 13 | FUTURE SHOCK | `Dalam [periode], [hal] akan [perubahan drastis]` | Evidence-based forecasts; label uncertainty. |
| 14 | IDENTITY CRISIS | `Kamu pikir kamu [identitas positif], padahal [realita]` | Challenge self-image carefully, without insulting the audience. |
| 15 | DOMINO EFFECT | `Satu [aksi kecil] memicu [efek berantai besar]` | Show cause-and-effect chains and compounding consequences. |

## Content-to-context method

1. **Feature/content** — what literally happened or what the topic states.
2. **Benefit or pain** — why it matters immediately.
3. **Meaning** — the deeper consequence for the specific audience.
4. **Context** — the one-sentence interpretation that gives the information life.
5. **So what** — continue the consequence chain until a meaningful tension appears.
6. **Plot-twist test** — compare the normal expectation `A → B` with the defensible surprising outcome `A → C`.

The surprising outcome must be explainable in the content. A contradiction without a credible payoff is clickbait.

## Simplification rules

- Remove greetings, setup, duplicated meaning, and words implied by context.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Keep carousel cover hooks at 15 words or fewer.
- A hook should be understood in under one second.
- Numbers must be exact enough to be credible and must have a source or be marked `[perlu verifikasi]`.
- Do not increase shock by overstating causality, risk, or certainty.
